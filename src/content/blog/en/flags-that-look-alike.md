Several pairs of national flags are almost impossible to tell apart, starting with Chad and Romania, whose flags are near identical. Shared colors, common history or pure coincidence: the reasons vary, but there is always one detail that gives each flag away. Here are 10 cases of flags that look alike, and how to tell them apart.

## 1. Chad and Romania

Both flags show the same vertical blue-yellow-red tricolor, to the point of being near identical. The only difference: Chad's blue is slightly darker than Romania's. Chad even took the matter to the UN in 2004 to contest the similarity, without result.

## 2. Indonesia and Monaco

Both flags are red over white, in two horizontal bands. Only the proportions differ: Monaco's flag is more square (4:5) than Indonesia's (2:3). Poland uses the same colors reversed, white over red.

## 3. Netherlands and Luxembourg

The same horizontal red-white-blue tricolor for both countries. Luxembourg's blue is noticeably lighter than the Dutch blue, and Luxembourg's flag is slightly more elongated.

## 4. Ireland and Ivory Coast

Both tricolors use the same colors, but mirrored: green sits at the hoist for Ireland, orange for Ivory Coast. The proportions also differ, Ireland's flag being more elongated.

## 5. Mali and Senegal

Two vertical green-yellow-red tricolors in Pan-African colors. Senegal adds a green star at the center of the yellow band, absent from Mali's flag. Guinea uses the same colors in reverse order.

## 6. Norway and Iceland

Two Nordic crosses with exactly inverted colors. Norway has a red field with a white-bordered blue cross; Iceland a blue field with a white-bordered red cross.

## 7. Australia and New Zealand

Both flags combine the Union Jack in the canton with the Southern Cross on a blue field. New Zealand has 4 red stars bordered in white; Australia has 6 white stars, including the large Commonwealth Star below the Union Jack.

## 8. Venezuela, Colombia and Ecuador

These three yellow-blue-red tricolors are inherited from the flag of Gran Colombia, the state founded by Simón Bolívar. Venezuela adds an arc of stars in the center, Ecuador places its coat of arms in the middle; Colombia's flag carries no emblem.

## 9. Slovenia, Slovakia and Russia

Three horizontal white-blue-red tricolors in Pan-Slavic colors. Tell them apart by the coat of arms: Slovenia places its own in the upper hoist corner, Slovakia its own toward the hoist, and Russia's flag carries none.

## 10. United States, Liberia and Malaysia

Three flags with red and white stripes and a canton in the upper hoist corner. Liberia, founded by former American slaves, has a single star in its blue canton; Malaysia places a crescent and a 14-pointed star on a blue field.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the two most similar flags in the world?

Those of Chad and Romania: two near-identical vertical blue-yellow-red tricolors. Only the shade of blue differs, slightly darker on Chad's side. Chad brought the matter before the UN in 2004, without result.

### Why do Monaco and Indonesia have the same flag?

It is a historical coincidence: both countries independently adopted a red-over-white flag, with no connection between them. They differ only in their proportions, Monaco's flag being more square (4:5) than Indonesia's (2:3).

### How do you tell the Australian and New Zealand flags apart?

Count the stars: New Zealand has 4 red stars bordered in white, Australia has 6 white stars. Australia also carries the large seven-pointed Commonwealth Star below the Union Jack.

### Why are there so many white-blue-red flags in Eastern Europe?

These are the Pan-Slavic colors, adopted in the 19th century by several Slavic peoples as a sign of solidarity. They were inspired by the Russian flag, hence the resemblance between the flags of Russia, Slovenia and Slovakia.
