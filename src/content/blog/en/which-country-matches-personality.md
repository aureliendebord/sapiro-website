Which country looks like you? Here is a playful 10-question test that crosses personality, values and culture to reveal the country that best matches you.

## The test

Note the letter of your answer to each question, then count your A, B, C and D answers at the end.

**1. Your relationship to time.**
- A) Always on time
- B) Flexible, depends on context
- C) Time is a suggestion

**2. Your relationship to nature.**
- A) I like nature but stay urban
- B) Nature is essential in my life
- C) I prefer urban comfort

**3. Your culinary style.**
- A) Refined cuisine, attention to detail
- B) Generous, convivial dishes
- C) Healthy and light
- D) Spicy and aromatic

**4. Your approach to work.**
- A) Discipline and structure
- B) Creativity and flexibility
- C) Work-life balance

**5. Your ideal weekend.**
- A) Cafe terrace and museum
- B) Mountain hiking
- C) Beach and lazy days
- D) Cultural outing with friends

**6. Your priority values.**
- A) Tradition and history
- B) Innovation and progress
- C) Family and community
- D) Individual freedom

**7. Your relationship to money.**
- A) Frugal and forward-looking
- B) Spendthrift and hedonistic
- C) Pragmatic and balanced

**8. Your ideal living place.**
- A) Big cosmopolitan city
- B) Seaside village
- C) Country house
- D) Mid-size historic city

**9. Your sport frequency.**
- A) Every day
- B) Several times a week
- C) Occasionally
- D) Rarely

**10. Your relationship to spirituality.**
- A) Very important
- B) Present but discreet
- C) Rather rationalist
- D) No interest

## Country profiles

Find the letter that comes up most often in your ten answers, then read the matching profile. If two letters tie, read both: you sit between two profiles. The letter D is only offered in six questions: if it comes first, or ties for first, go with profile D.

This is a game, with no scientific claim whatsoever. The countries are named for verifiable facts, not for the supposed character of their inhabitants.

**Mostly A: the refined planner. Your mirror countries: Japan and Switzerland.** Punctuality, attention to detail, a sense of tradition and of saving. In Japan, washoku, the traditional cuisine, has been on [UNESCO's intangible cultural heritage list](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/washoku-traditional-dietary-cultures-of-the-japanese-notably-for-the-celebration-of-new-year-00869) since 2013, among other things for the care given to the presentation of dishes. In Switzerland, the federal railways reported [94.1% of passenger trains on time in 2025](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-politics/swiss-federal-railways-reports-record-punctuality-in-2025/90842888), a record.

**Mostly B: the outdoor enthusiast. Your mirror countries: Norway, New Zealand and Finland.** Nature in everyday life, hiking, shared meals, a taste for new things. In Norway, the [right to roam](https://www.environmentagency.no/areas-of-activity/right-to-roam/about-right-to-roam/) lets everyone walk, ski and camp freely in open country. In New Zealand, the Department of Conservation regulates activity on [about one third of the country's land](https://www.doc.govt.nz/get-involved/apply-for-permits/how-we-regulate/). Finland ranks first in the [World Happiness Report](https://data.worldhappiness.report/table) published in 2026, and Norway sixth.

**Mostly C: the balance seeker. Your mirror countries: Denmark, the Netherlands and Costa Rica.** Time for yourself, for family and for the beach, without racing the clock. Denmark and the Netherlands are among the OECD countries with [the fewest hours worked per year](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/indicators/hours-worked.html) (2023 data, which the OECD advises comparing across countries with caution). In the World Happiness Report published in 2026, Denmark ranks third, Costa Rica fourth and the Netherlands seventh.

**Mostly D: the free spirit. Your mirror countries: Czechia and Mexico.** Individual freedom, cultural outings with friends, historic cities on a human scale, spicy food. In Czechia, [72% of adults](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2017/06/19/unlike-their-central-and-eastern-european-neighbors-most-czechs-dont-believe-in-god/) said they did not identify with any religion in a Pew Research Center survey published in 2017. In Mexico, traditional cuisine, based on corn, beans and chili, has been on [UNESCO's intangible cultural heritage list](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-mexican-cuisine-ancestral-ongoing-community-culture-the-michoacan-paradigm-00400) since 2010. The country ranks twelfth in the World Happiness Report published in 2026.

## How to interpret

The mirror country is not necessarily where you would want to live. It is the one whose dominant cultural values resonate most with your personality.

Many people land on a profile they did not expect. National culture is not a biological destiny.

## To go further

The mirror country revealed by the test can give you a travel idea: a good opportunity to check on the spot whether the portrait fits.

SAPIRO offers quizzes to better know the 197 countries of the world, their culture, history, geography. Worth reading: [least visited countries in the world](/en/blog/least-visited-countries-world/) and [origin of country names](/en/blog/origin-country-names/).

This test is intentionally simplified and playful. Cultural stereotypes are starting points, never absolute truths. Each country holds millions of different personalities.
