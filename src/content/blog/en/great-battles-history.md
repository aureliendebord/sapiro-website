A few battles changed the course of the world. Here are the 15 most decisive military clashes in world history, in chronological order.

## Antiquity

**Marathon (-490).** The Athenians beat the Persians. Saves the nascent Greek democracy. Origin of the marathon race: according to legend, a messenger ran from Marathon to Athens, about 40 km, to announce victory. The official distance of [42.195 km](https://www.aims-worldrunning.org/articles/678-going-the-distance.html) dates back to the 1908 London Games.

**Gaugamela (-331).** Alexander the Great crushes Darius III. Collapse of the Persian Empire.

**Cannae (-216).** Hannibal annihilates the Roman army. Envelopment tactic still taught today in military schools.

**Actium (-31).** Octavian beats Antony and Cleopatra. He is left sole master of Rome, which opens the way to the Roman Empire.

## Middle Ages

**Tours (732).** Near Poitiers, Charles Martel defeats the army of the Umayyad governor of Córdoba. The defeat marks the end of medieval Muslim expansion in the West, even though the Umayyads hold Narbonne until 759, as the [Encyclopædia Universalis](https://www.universalis.fr/encyclopedie/bataille-de-poitiers/) points out.

**Hastings (1066).** William the Conqueror beats Harold. Normandy takes control of England. The Bayeux Tapestry tells the story.

**Agincourt (1415).** Henry V of England crushes the more numerous French. Demonstration of the power of English archers.

## Early modern

**Constantinople (1453).** The Ottomans take the Byzantine capital. End of the Eastern Roman Empire, major cultural shock.

**Lepanto (1571).** The Christian coalition beats the Ottomans at sea. A check, at least a symbolic one, on Ottoman expansion in the Mediterranean.

**Trafalgar (1805).** Nelson crushes the Franco-Spanish fleet. British naval dominance for a century.

**Waterloo (1815).** On June 18, [Wellington and Blücher defeat Napoleon](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/battle-waterloo). End of the Napoleonic era.

## Contemporary era

**Verdun (1916).** One of the longest and deadliest battles of World War I: ten months of fighting, from February 21 to December 18, 1916, about 306,000 dead and missing (163,000 French, 143,000 German) and 406,000 wounded, according to the [Verdun Memorial](https://memorial-verdun.fr/files/6a80fff1/le_bilan_des_batailles_de_verdun_et_de_la_somme.pdf). The Somme, the same year, caused even more losses: over a million men killed, wounded, missing or captured. French symbol of trench warfare.

**Stalingrad (1942-1943).** Turning point of World War II on the Eastern Front. First crushing defeat of the Wehrmacht against the USSR: Field Marshal Paulus surrenders on January 31, 1943. According to the [Deutsches Historisches Museum](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/kapitel/der-zweite-weltkrieg/kriegsverlauf/schlacht-um-stalingrad-194243.html), about 150,000 German soldiers die in the pocket, 91,000 are taken prisoner, and Soviet losses probably exceed 400,000 men.

**Normandy landings (June 6, 1944).** Largest amphibious invasion in military history: almost 133,000 Allied troops land that day, according to the [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/research/online-documents/world-war-ii-d-day-invasion-normandy). Opens a new front in the west against Nazi Germany.

**Dien Bien Phu (1954).** The French entrenched camp [falls on May 7, 1954](https://www.larousse.fr/encyclopedie/divers/bataille_de_Di%C3%AAn_Bi%C3%AAn_Phu/116476) to the Viet Minh. The Geneva Accords, in July, end the Indochina War. Decolonization accelerates.

## How to remember

Tie each battle to a figure. Marathon = no one (yet famous). Hastings = William the Conqueror. Waterloo = Napoleon. Stalingrad = no individual general, but the Soviet symbol.

Think about consequences more than military details. A battle matters to history by what it changes. Constantinople tips the Middle Ages into the Renaissance. Stalingrad tips the fate of Nazism.

Visualize on a map. Every battle happens somewhere. Geography shapes the result.
