The 20th century saw dictatorships take hold on every continent. Here are the major authoritarian regimes, their ideologies and consequences, explained to understand without oversimplifying.

## Fascisms (1920-1945)

**Benito Mussolini (Italy, 1922-1943).** Founder of fascism. Appointed head of government after the March on Rome in October 1922. First model of modern totalitarian dictatorship. Allied with Hitler from 1936. Deposed and arrested in July 1943, he then ran the Italian Social Republic from Salò with German backing. Shot by partisans on April 28, 1945.

**Adolf Hitler (Germany, 1933-1945).** Chancellor in 1933, total dictator after 1934. Ideology: nationalism, antisemitic racism, territorial expansion (Lebensraum). Responsible for World War II and the [Holocaust](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/documenting-numbers-of-victims-of-the-holocaust-and-nazi-persecution): six million Jews murdered, according to the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum. The war killed about 15 million combatants and 45 million civilians worldwide, according to the [National WWII Museum](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/students-teachers/student-resources/research-starters/research-starters-worldwide-deaths-world-war), which notes that estimates vary widely between sources. Suicide in 1945.

**Francisco Franco (Spain, 1939-1975).** Head of state of the Nationalist side from 1938, he takes control of all of Spain at the end of the Civil War (1936-1939). Conservative, Catholic, anti-communist regime. One of the longest European dictatorships of the 20th century: 36 years, until his death on November 20, 1975.

**António de Oliveira Salazar (Portugal, 1932-1968).** Prime minister from July 1932, he founds a conservative authoritarian regime, the Estado Novo. He governs for 36 years, until a stroke removes him from power in 1968. His regime outlives him and falls with the Carnation Revolution in 1974.

## Authoritarian communisms

**Joseph Stalin (USSR, 1924-1953).** Lenin's successor. Forced industrialization, agricultural collectivization. Gulag. Great Terror of 1937-1938: 682,691 people executed. The famine of 1930-1933 kills more than five million people in the USSR, including about 3.3 million in Soviet Ukraine (Holodomor). In total, historian [Timothy Snyder](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2011/03/10/hitler-vs-stalin-who-killed-more/) attributes to the regime about six million civilians deliberately killed, nine million when foreseeable deaths from famine and the camps are included. Before the Soviet archives opened, estimates ran to twenty million or more.

**Mao Zedong (China, 1949-1976).** Founder of the People's Republic. Great Leap Forward (1958-1961): the famine it causes kills between 30 and 45 million people according to [researchers' estimates](https://www.nybooks.com/daily/2018/02/05/who-killed-more-hitler-stalin-or-mao/) (30 million for demographer Judith Banister, 45 million for historian Frank Dikötter). Cultural Revolution (1966-1976): 1.1 to 3 million deaths according to historians, and millions of lives broken.

**Kim Il-sung and the Kim dynasty (North Korea, 1948-).** Family dictatorship still running. One of the most closed countries in the world. Major famine in the 1990s: between 600,000 and 1 million deaths from 1995 to 2000 according to demographers [Daniel Goodkind and Loraine West](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/popdev/v27y2001i2p219-238.html), while other assessments range from 200,000 to 3 million.

**Fidel Castro (Cuba, 1959-2008).** Communist revolution about 150 km from the United States. One-party regime. No mass killings on the scale of Stalin's or Mao's, but repression of opponents and lasting economic and political deprivations.

## Dictatorships of the Global South

**Pol Pot (Cambodia, 1975-1979).** Khmer Rouge. Attempt to erase modern society and return to pure agriculture. About 1.7 million deaths, or 21% of the population, according to [Yale University's Cambodian Genocide Program](https://macmillan.yale.edu/node/3003267/cambodian-genocide-program).

**Augusto Pinochet (Chile, 1973-1990).** Coup of September 11, 1973 against Salvador Allende. The United States had run covert actions in Chile, but whether it directly supported the coup [is still debated](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1969-1976/allende). The Chilean state has recognized 3,216 dead and disappeared and 38,254 survivors of political imprisonment and torture ([2011 count](https://derechoshumanos.udp.cl/cms/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Cifras-DD-EP-exPP-reconocidos-a-Ago2013.pdf)). Lost a referendum in 1988, democratic transition.

**Mobutu (Zaire/DRC, 1965-1997).** Thirty-two years in power. Massive economic plunder (kleptocracy). Died in exile after being overthrown.

**Saddam Hussein (Iraq, 1979-2003).** Iran-Iraq War (1980-1988), Gulf War (1990-1991). Overthrown by the US invasion in 2003. Executed in 2006.

## What they have in common

Three traits keep coming back.

**Cult of personality.** All systematized a staging of their own figure: portraits everywhere, official biographies, slogans, statues. Visual propaganda is central.

**Single-party rule or personal power without checks.** No legal opposition, rigged or canceled elections, controlled press, ever-present political police.

**The construction of an internal enemy.** Jews for Hitler, "enemies of the people" for Stalin, "intellectuals" and "exploiters" for Mao and Pol Pot, communists for Pinochet and Franco. This designated enemy justifies repression and cements the regime.

## Why study these regimes

Three reasons.

To understand how democracies tip. Almost all of these regimes took hold from within: through elections, coups, revolutions or civil wars. Understanding this tipping is learning to prevent it.

To grasp the human cost. The numbers are abstract. But some 60 million deaths for World War II, six to nine million civilians killed under Stalin, 30 to 45 million famine deaths under Mao: these numbers exceed understanding and force an effort of imagination to make them concrete.

To understand the 21st century. Many current tensions (China-Taiwan, Russia-Ukraine, Koreas) are direct legacies of 20th-century dictatorships.
