The least visited countries in the world sometimes receive fewer than 5,000 tourists a year. This ranking relies on a single series: [international tourist arrivals](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/ST.INT.ARVL) compiled by UN Tourism and published by the World Bank (indicator ST.INT.ARVL). The year used is 2019, the last before the pandemic and the last for which the series covers most countries. Only UN member states are ranked, and several very rarely visited countries are missing because no figure is published: they are covered further down.

## 1. Tuvalu (3,600 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/tv.svg" alt="Flag of Tuvalu" loading="lazy" />

The least visited country among those that publish a figure: [3,600 arrivals in 2019](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/ST.INT.ARVL?locations=TV), after 3,200 in 2018. The [fourth smallest country in the world](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tuvalu), it has nine coral islands in the Pacific, 26 km² and about 9,600 inhabitants in 2024 according to the World Bank.

**Why so few visitors?** Difficult access (air links run through Fiji and Kiribati), limited tourism infrastructure, high prices.

## 2. Marshall Islands (6,100 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/mh.svg" alt="Flag of Marshall Islands" loading="lazy" />

[29 atolls and 5 coral islands](https://www.britannica.com/place/Marshall-Islands) in the Pacific. Population: about 37,500 in 2024.

**Why so few?** Very isolated, US nuclear testing legacy, limited infrastructure.

## 3. Kiribati (12,000 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/ki.svg" alt="Flag of Kiribati" loading="lazy" />

[33 islands, only 20 of them inhabited](https://www.britannica.com/place/Kiribati), scattered over a vast stretch of the Pacific on both sides of the equator.

**Why so few?** Vast distances between islands, access difficulty, sea level rise.

**What to see.** Its easternmost islands are among the first places on Earth to enter each new day.

## 4. Micronesia (18,000 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/fm.svg" alt="Flag of Micronesia" loading="lazy" />

More than 600 islands and islets of the Caroline archipelago, divided into four states: Yap, Chuuk, Pohnpei and Kosrae.

**Why so few?** Remoteness and scattered islands. Arrivals are falling: 42,100 in 2013, 18,000 in 2019.

## 5. Solomon Islands (28,900 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/sb.svg" alt="Flag of Solomon Islands" loading="lazy" />

A double chain of volcanic islands and coral atolls in Melanesia. Capital: Honiara, on Guadalcanal.

## 6. Comoros (45,100 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/km.svg" alt="Flag of Comoros" loading="lazy" />

Indian Ocean archipelago between the coast of Mozambique and Madagascar.

**Why so few?** Limited infrastructure and low profile. Arrivals were growing, though: 23,600 in 2015, 45,100 in 2019.

## 7. Guinea-Bissau (52,400 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/gw.svg" alt="Flag of Guinea-Bissau" loading="lazy" />

Former Portuguese colony in West Africa, extended offshore by the Bijagós archipelago.

## 8. Sierra Leone (71,000 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/sl.svg" alt="Flag of Sierra Leone" loading="lazy" />

Coastal West African country between Guinea and Liberia. Capital: Freetown.

## 9. Timor-Leste (74,800 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/tl.svg" alt="Flag of Timor-Leste" loading="lazy" />

The eastern half of the island of Timor, in Southeast Asia. Independent since 2002, it is one of the youngest states in the world.

## 10. Chad (81,000 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/td.svg" alt="Flag of Chad" loading="lazy" />

Landlocked Central African country covering 1.28 million km².

## 11. Central African Republic (87,000 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/cf.svg" alt="Flag of Central African Republic" loading="lazy" />

Landlocked country at the heart of the African continent, 623,000 km².

## 12. Palau and Tonga, tied (94,000 tourists in 2019)

### Palau
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/pw.svg" alt="Flag of Palau" loading="lazy" />

Some 340 coral and volcanic islands in the western Pacific. With fewer than 18,000 inhabitants, the archipelago receives five times its population in visitors: little visited in absolute terms, heavily visited for its size.

### Tonga
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/to.svg" alt="Flag of Tonga" loading="lazy" />

Polynesian kingdom of some 170 islands in three main groups: Tongatapu, Ha'apai and Vava'u.

## 14. Liechtenstein (98,100 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/li.svg" alt="Flag of Liechtenstein" loading="lazy" />

Microstate between Switzerland and Austria. 160 km².

**Why few despite sitting in the heart of Europe?** The statistic counts visitors who stay at least one night. Day-trippers from Switzerland or Austria are not included.

## 15. Burkina Faso (143,000 tourists in 2019)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/bf.svg" alt="Flag of Burkina Faso" loading="lazy" />

Landlocked Sahel country in West Africa, 274,200 km².

## Countries missing from the ranking for lack of data

Several countries probably receive fewer visitors than some of the fifteen above, but no reliable figure is published for 2019. Ranking them would mean inventing numbers.

### Nauru (Naoero)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/nr.svg" alt="Flag of Nauru" loading="lazy" />

The [third smallest country in the world](https://www.britannica.com/place/Naoero) (21 km², about 12,000 inhabitants). The World Bank series contains no value for Nauru. The figures found online (often "200 tourists a year") do not trace back to any published statistic. Phosphate mining has ravaged the interior of the island, leaving about four-fifths of it uninhabitable.

### Turkmenistan
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/tm.svg" alt="Flag of Turkmenistan" loading="lazy" />

Central Asian republic, formerly part of the USSR, with a closed regime. No figure in the series. Visas are reputedly hard to obtain. What to see: Ashgabat with its white marble buildings, the ["Darvaza Crater"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darvaza_gas_crater), a gas pit that has been burning for decades.

### Sao Tome and Principe
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/st.svg" alt="Flag of Sao Tome and Principe" loading="lazy" />

Two Portuguese-speaking islands off the coast of Gabon. Last published figure: 33,400 arrivals in 2018, which would place it around sixth. Beaches, cocoa plantations, tropical biodiversity.

### Eritrea
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/er.svg" alt="Flag of Eritrea" loading="lazy" />

On the Horn of Africa, under an authoritarian regime. Last published figure: 142,000 arrivals in 2016.

**Others.** Afghanistan, North Korea, Equatorial Guinea, Libya, Somalia and South Sudan have no value in the series. In North Korea, the few foreign visitors travel on escorted tours.

## Two countries often wrongly listed

### Bhutan
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/bt.svg" alt="Flag of Bhutan" loading="lazy" />

The Himalayan kingdom is thought of as an almost closed destination. Yet it recorded 316,000 arrivals in 2019, more than twice as many as Burkina Faso, fifteenth in this ranking. It manages tourism through a nightly levy, the "Sustainable Development Fee", cut from $200 to $100 in September 2023. What to see: Himalayan monasteries, including the famous Tiger's Nest.

### Algeria
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/dz.svg" alt="Flag of Algeria" loading="lazy" />

The largest country in Africa received 2.4 million visitors in 2019. That is few for its size, but in a different league from the countries ranked here.

## Countries recently opened to tourism

**Saudi Arabia.** Electronic tourist visa launched in September 2019.

**Iran.** Open to tourism, but Western sanctions complicate travel.

## Why visit these countries?

Three reasons.

**Alternative tourism.** Far from crowds, authentic experience.

**Direct economic support.** When a country receives few tourists, each visit really counts.

**Geopolitical curiosity.** Understanding less-mediated cultures.

## Precautions

**Documentation.** Check visas, vaccines, security.

**Respect.** Responsible tourism, do not disturb local communities.

**Preparation.** No massive infrastructure, plan for self-sufficiency.

At the other end of the scale, the [most visited countries in the world](/en/blog/most-visited-countries-world/) each receive more tourists in a single day than Tuvalu does in a year.
