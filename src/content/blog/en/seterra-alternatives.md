If you want apps like Seterra, five are worth trying: SAPIRO for flag, capital and map quizzes that explain every answer and carry no ads, StudyGe for its interactive atlas, World Geography for country fact cards, GeoGuessr for exploration, and Stack the Countries for kids. None of them beats Seterra at its own game, which is detailed blank-map drilling. They cover what Seterra leaves out.

Fair warning: this is SAPIRO's website and we make the app. That is why it comes first below, and we also say where the others do a better job.

## Why people look beyond Seterra

Seterra does one thing very well. It shows you a map and you click the right spot: countries, capitals and flags, but also regions, federal states, rivers, mountains, deserts and lakes. If your goal is to master the world map in detail, it is the reference, and our [SAPIRO vs Seterra comparison](/en/blog/sapiro-vs-seterra/) says so plainly.

The reasons to look elsewhere tend to be the same. It is geography only. The format rarely moves away from map-clicking against a score and a timer. And the interface looks dated next to newer apps.

## 1. SAPIRO: flags, capitals and maps, with the explanation included

SAPIRO is a general knowledge quiz where geography is one of four worlds, next to history, art and nature. On the geography side it covers all 197 countries through flags, capitals and maps. Every answer comes with a short explanation, so a wrong guess teaches you something instead of just costing a point. The free version has no ads. The app is on iOS and Android.

If you mostly sit at a computer, this is the useful part: you can [play free in your browser](/en/play/) with nothing to install. There is a Daily Challenge, free and identical for everyone, and you can share your score to dare a friend to beat it.

Where it falls short: on pure geography SAPIRO is less exhaustive than Seterra. You will not be placing rivers one by one or clicking federal states on a blank map. If that is what you are after, keep Seterra.

## 2. StudyGe: the interactive atlas

StudyGe is the closest in spirit to Seterra, because everything starts from the map. You browse the world, tap a country to see its capital, population, area and currency, then test yourself. There is a flag quiz and a gradual learn mode that introduces new countries step by step. The map is the star, and it works offline.

The quiz modes feel basic next to dedicated quiz apps, some content is unlocked through in-app purchases, and it stays on pure geography, so cultural knowledge gets little attention.

## 3. World Geography: fact cards and quick drills

World Geography is a no-nonsense quiz app covering countries, capitals, flags, populations and currencies, with a standard quiz, a time attack and a learning mode. It is lightweight, loads fast, tracks your progress by category and works offline. Handy for a few minutes on a commute.

The tradeoffs: the free version is heavy on ads, the interface feels generic and questions can get repetitive. If you are trying to get away from ads, this will not fix it.

## 4. GeoGuessr: work out where you are

GeoGuessr drops you into a random Google Street View location and asks you to figure out where on Earth you are. Road signs, vegetation, architecture, the position of the sun: everything is a clue. Multiplayer modes and themed maps keep it fresh, and you build observation skills no flashcard app can teach.

The learning is indirect, though. There is no list of capitals to drill, Street View coverage is uneven in parts of Africa and Central Asia, the free tier limits how much you can play, and you need a constant connection. More in our [SAPIRO vs GeoGuessr comparison](/en/blog/sapiro-vs-geoguessr/).

## 5. Stack the Countries: for ages 6 to 12

Answer a geography question correctly and you earn a country shape to stack on a platform. Stack high enough to cross the line and you win the round. It is geography meets Tetris, and with younger players it works surprisingly well. It teaches country shapes along with facts, and there are no ads and no in-app purchases once you have bought it.

It is a paid app, adults will find it too simple, and the content does not update often. For more ideas with children, see our guide to [geography for kids](/en/blog/geography-for-kids/).

## Which one should you pick?

**You want to understand, not just place pins.** SAPIRO. The explanation after each answer is what makes a flag stick, and the three other worlds keep things from getting repetitive. Our guide to [the hardest flags to recognize](/en/blog/hardest-flags/) is a good warm-up.

**You are on a computer and do not want to install anything.** The [SAPIRO browser game](/en/play/) for quizzes, GeoGuessr for exploration. Seterra's web version stays the right tool for the map itself.

**You live for the map.** StudyGe, or simply stay with Seterra. For pure location drilling nobody has done it better.

**You want two minutes of practice on the bus.** World Geography, if the ads do not bother you.

**You are choosing for a child.** Stack the Countries between 6 and 12.

Nothing says you have to delete Seterra. Plenty of people keep one app for the map and another for short quizzes. For a wider roundup that also covers Google Earth and a few others, see our pick of the [best geography apps](/en/blog/best-geography-apps/).
