Several countries split their institutions between two capitals, and South Africa between three. The pattern holds from one case to the next: the executive, the parliament and sometimes the judiciary do not sit in the same city. The reasons vary. Sometimes a division inherited from history, sometimes an administrative capital built to relieve the older one. Ten countries split their capital across several cities this way.

## 1. South Africa (Pretoria, Cape Town, Bloemfontein)

The country with [three capitals](https://www.gov.za/about-sa/south-africa-glance): Pretoria hosts the executive, Cape Town the Parliament and Bloemfontein the judiciary. This arrangement dates back to the creation of the Union of South Africa in 1910.

## 2. Bolivia (Sucre, La Paz)

Sucre is the constitutional capital and home to the Supreme Court, but the government and parliament have sat in La Paz since 1898. Perched at around 3,600 metres, La Paz is the [highest national capital in the world](https://www.britannica.com/place/La-Paz-Bolivia).

## 3. Netherlands (Amsterdam, The Hague)

[Named in the constitution](https://www.government.nl/topics/constitution/documents/reports/2019/02/28/the-constitution-of-the-kingdom-of-the-netherlands) (Article 32), Amsterdam nonetheless hosts no political institutions. The government, the parliament, the royal residence and the international courts of justice all sit in The Hague.

## 4. Sri Lanka (Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte, Colombo)

Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte is the legislative capital: [parliament has sat there since April 1982](https://parliament.lk/en/handbook-of-parliament/evolution-of-the-parliamentary-system). Nearby Colombo remains the seat of the executive and the judiciary, as well as the country's economic capital.

## 5. Malaysia (Kuala Lumpur, Putrajaya)

Kuala Lumpur remains the official capital and the seat of parliament. Putrajaya, a planned city built in the 1990s, hosts the federal administration: the prime minister's office moved there in 1999.

## 6. Tanzania (Dodoma, Dar es Salaam)

Dodoma, in the centre of the country, was [designated the capital in 1973](https://www.britannica.com/place/Dodoma) and hosts the parliament; the transfer of other institutions has stretched over several decades. Dar es Salaam, the former capital on the coast, remains the economic centre and the country's largest city.

## 7. Ivory Coast (Yamoussoukro, Abidjan)

Yamoussoukro, the birthplace of President Houphouët-Boigny, has been the [political capital since 1983](https://www.britannica.com/place/Yamoussoukro). Abidjan remains the economic capital and the de facto seat of government and embassies.

## 8. Benin (Porto-Novo, Cotonou)

Porto-Novo is the official capital, but Cotonou, the largest city and chief port, serves as the de facto administrative capital.

## 9. Eswatini (Mbabane, Lobamba)

Mbabane is the administrative capital, seat of the government. Lobamba, for its part, is the [royal and legislative capital](https://www.britannica.com/place/Lobamba): it hosts the parliament and the residence of the queen mother.

## 10. Chile (Santiago, Valparaíso)

Santiago is the capital and concentrates the executive and the judiciary. The National Congress, however, has sat in Valparaíso on the Pacific coast [since March 11, 1990](https://www.bcn.cl/historiapolitica/congreso_nacional/edificios/detalle?sel=Edificio_Congreso_Nacional_%28Valpara%C3%ADso%29).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which country has three capitals?

South Africa has three capitals: Pretoria (executive), Cape Town (parliament) and Bloemfontein (judiciary). The arrangement dates back to the creation of the Union of South Africa in 1910.

### What is the capital of Bolivia?

Sucre is the constitutional capital of Bolivia, but the government and parliament sit in La Paz. The two cities therefore share the title, with La Paz being the highest seat of government in the world.

### What is the capital of the Netherlands?

Amsterdam is the capital of the Netherlands according to the constitution. The government, the parliament and the royal family, however, are based in The Hague, which also hosts the international courts of justice.

### Why do some countries have more than one capital?

Often for historical reasons, when several cities ended up sharing the branches of government, as in South Africa or Bolivia. Other countries deliberately separate the administration from the historic capital, as Malaysia did with Putrajaya or Tanzania with Dodoma.
