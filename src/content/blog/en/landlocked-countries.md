[44 countries](https://www.britannica.com/topic/landlocked-country), including 2 doubly landlocked ones, have no direct access to the ocean. They are the landlocked states. The UN classes 32 of them as [landlocked developing countries](https://www.un.org/ohrlls/content/about-landlocked-developing-countries). Without a coast of their own, each one relies on a neighbour to reach a port and ship its goods abroad. Some lost their coastline in a war; many never had one. Here are ten that stand out.

## 1. Kazakhstan (the largest landlocked country)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/kz.svg" alt="Flag of Kazakhstan" loading="lazy" />


At around 2.7 million km², Kazakhstan is the largest landlocked country in the world. It does border the Caspian Sea, a closed sea that opens onto no ocean.

## 2. Ethiopia (the most populous landlocked country)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/et.svg" alt="Flag of Ethiopia" loading="lazy" />


With [132 million inhabitants in 2024](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL?locations=ET) according to the World Bank, Ethiopia is the most populous landlocked country in the world. It lost its coastline in 1993, when Eritrea became independent. Since then, [more than 95% of its imports and exports](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/783131679677502935/txt/Ethiopia-Trade-Logistics-Project.txt) have passed through the port of Djibouti, according to the World Bank.

## 3. Bolivia (a navy without a sea)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/bo.svg" alt="Flag of Bolivia" loading="lazy" />


Bolivia lost its coastline to Chile in the War of the Pacific (1879-1883). It still maintains a navy, which trains on Lake Titicaca.

## 4. Liechtenstein (doubly landlocked)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/li.svg" alt="Flag of Liechtenstein" loading="lazy" />


One of only two doubly landlocked countries in the world. Its two neighbours, Switzerland and Austria, are themselves landlocked: you must cross at least two borders to reach the sea.

## 5. Uzbekistan (the other doubly landlocked country)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/uz.svg" alt="Flag of Uzbekistan" loading="lazy" />


The planet's second doubly landlocked country. It is surrounded by five states that have no sea access themselves.

## 6. Mongolia (only two neighbours)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/mn.svg" alt="Flag of Mongolia" loading="lazy" />


The second-largest landlocked country in the world. Mongolia is wedged between just two neighbours: Russia to the north and China to the south.

## 7. Switzerland (a merchant fleet without a coast)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/ch.svg" alt="Flag of Switzerland" loading="lazy" />


Switzerland has no coast, yet [merchant ships fly the Swiss flag](https://www.schweizerischesseeschifffahrtsamt.eda.admin.ch/en/commercial-maritime-shipping) on the world's oceans, run by shipping companies based in the country. It reaches the North Sea along the Rhine, from the river port of Basel.

## 8. Nepal (the roof of the world)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/np.svg" alt="Flag of Nepal" loading="lazy" />


Hemmed in between India and China, Nepal is home to [eight peaks above 8,000 metres](https://ntb.gov.np/things-to-do/mountaineering), including Everest.

## 9. Paraguay (access through rivers)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/py.svg" alt="Flag of Paraguay" loading="lazy" />


Paraguay has no coastline, but a vast river frontage connects it to the Atlantic. The Itaipú dam, shared with Brazil, is [one of the most powerful in the world](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Itaipu-Dam), at 14,000 megawatts.

## 10. Vatican City (the smallest landlocked country)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/va.svg" alt="Flag of Vatican City" loading="lazy" />


The smallest landlocked country in the world, at 0.44 km². It is enclosed within a single city: Rome.

Vatican City and Liechtenstein are among the [smallest countries in the world](/en/blog/smallest-countries-world/), Liechtenstein also counts as one of the [European microstates](/en/blog/european-microstates/), and the tangle of their land borders produces some of the [strangest borders on the planet](/en/blog/strangest-borders-world/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many countries have no access to the sea?

44 countries have no direct access to the sea. That total includes two doubly landlocked countries, Liechtenstein and Uzbekistan. States bordering the Caspian Sea, a closed sea, are counted as landlocked.

### What is the largest landlocked country in the world?

Kazakhstan is the largest landlocked country, at around 2.7 million km². It borders the Caspian Sea, but that is a closed sea with no access to the ocean. Mongolia comes second.

### Which are the two doubly landlocked countries?

Liechtenstein and Uzbekistan are the only two doubly landlocked countries. A doubly landlocked country is surrounded exclusively by states that are themselves landlocked: you must cross at least two borders to reach a coast.

### Why does Bolivia have a navy?

Bolivia lost its coastline to Chile in the War of the Pacific (1879-1883), but has never given up its maritime claim. It maintains a navy, which trains on Lake Titicaca.
