About ten territories function today as states (a government, a currency, sometimes an army) without a seat at the UN. International recognition among them is very uneven: around a hundred countries for some, a single one or none for others. The ten cases below set out where each one stands.

## 1. Taiwan (recognized by about a dozen states)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/tw.svg" alt="Flag of Taiwan" loading="lazy" />


Around 23 million inhabitants, its own government since 1949, a currency and an army. Taiwan is recognized by only [12 states](https://en.mofa.gov.tw/AlliesIndex.aspx?n=1294&sms=1007) as of November 2026: 11 UN members and the Holy See. China considers it one of its provinces.

## 2. Kosovo (recognized by about a hundred countries)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/xk.svg" alt="Flag of Kosovo" loading="lazy" />


Kosovo declared independence from Serbia in 2008. Around a hundred countries recognize it ([108 recognitions](https://mfa-ks.net/lista-e-njohjeve/) listed by its foreign ministry as of November 2026), but it is not a UN member. Serbia still considers it part of its territory.

## 3. Palestine (UN observer state)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/countries/ps.svg" alt="Flag of Palestine" loading="lazy" />


Palestine has been a non-member observer state at the UN since 2012. More than 150 countries recognize it as a state: [157 of the 193 UN member states](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/9/23/which-are-the-150-countries-that-have-recognised-palestine-as-of-2025) as of late September 2025, after recognitions by France, the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia. Its borders and final status remain the subject of unresolved negotiations.

## 4. Western Sahara (unresolved status)

The Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR), proclaimed in 1976, has been a [member of the African Union](https://au.int/en/member_states/countryprofiles2) since 1982. The territory is mostly administered by Morocco, which claims it. The status of Western Sahara remains unresolved at the UN: in October 2025, [Security Council resolution 2797](https://mandates.un.org/mandate/S/RES/2797%282025%29) called for negotiations taking Morocco's autonomy proposal as a basis.

## 5. Northern Cyprus (recognized only by Turkey)

The Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus was proclaimed in 1983, in the north of an island divided since 1974. It is recognized only by Turkey. The rest of the international community considers the area part of the Republic of Cyprus.

## 6. Somaliland (recognized by Israel since 2025)

Somaliland declared its independence from Somalia in 1991. It has its own institutions, its own currency, and holds regular elections. For more than thirty years no UN member state recognized it, until Israel [became the first to do so on December 26, 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/12/26/israel-becomes-first-country-to-recognise-somaliland). Somalia, the African Union and most countries still consider it part of Somalia.

## 7. Transnistria (de facto secession)

A strip of land between Moldova and Ukraine, a self-proclaimed republic since 1990 and in de facto secession ever since. Transnistria has its own government, currency and documents. The international community considers it part of Moldova.

## 8. Abkhazia (limited recognition)

Abkhazia, which declared independence in 1999, has been recognized by Russia since 2008, following the conflict between Russia and Georgia. Since [Nauru withdrew its recognition in July 2026](https://meduza.io/en/news/2026/07/30/a-nation-of-10-000-people-just-shrank-the-list-of-countries-recognizing-abkhazia-and-south-ossetia-to-four), only four UN member states recognize it: Russia, Nicaragua, Venezuela and Syria. The international community considers it part of Georgia. In practice it operates with its own institutions.

## 9. South Ossetia (limited recognition)

South Ossetia's situation has been similar to Abkhazia's since 2008: recognized by the same four states, considered Georgian by the rest of the international community. The territory has around 53,000 inhabitants (2014).

## 10. Nagorno-Karabakh (dissolved in 2024)

The self-proclaimed republic of Artsakh, populated mostly by Armenians, announced its dissolution as of January 1, 2024, in a [decree signed on September 28, 2023](https://www.rferl.org/a/armenia-azerbaijan-separatists-decree/32743775.html), after Azerbaijan retook the territory. Its leaders in exile later declared the decree void, but the republic no longer administers any territory. It is the most recent case of a de facto state that ceased to exist.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many countries are not recognized by the UN?

About ten territories function as states without being UN members. Their degree of recognition varies widely: more than 150 countries for Palestine, about a hundred for Kosovo, a single one for Northern Cyprus (Turkey) and for Somaliland (Israel), none for Transnistria.

### Why is Taiwan not a member of the UN?

In 1971, UN Resolution 2758 assigned China's seat to the People's Republic of China, and Taiwan lost its own. Since then, China has considered Taiwan one of its provinces and opposes any membership. Taiwan remains recognized by 12 states, including 11 UN members.

### Which country is recognized by absolutely no one?

Transnistria. No UN member state recognizes it, only Abkhazia and South Ossetia, which are themselves barely recognized. Somaliland long held this record too, until Israel recognized it in December 2025.

### Is Kosovo a country?

Kosovo has been recognized as a state by around a hundred countries since its declaration of independence in 2008, but it is not a UN member. Serbia still considers it part of its territory, and both positions coexist on the international stage.
