The largest empire in history is the British Empire, with about 35.5 million km² at its peak in 1920, a little over a quarter of the world's land area outside Antarctica. Next come the Mongols, then the Russians, before the gaps start to narrow. The areas used here are those of political scientist Rein Taagepera, who measured the territory each empire actually controlled ([1997 study](https://escholarship.org/content/qt3cn68807/qt3cn68807.pdf)); for earlier empires they remain estimates. The ranking below covers the top ten, by maximum land area.

## 1. British Empire (~35.5 M km², 1920)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/empires/british.svg" alt="Flag of British Empire" loading="lazy" />


At its peak, just after the First World War, the British Empire covered about 35.5 million km², a little over a quarter of the 133 million km² of land outside Antarctica. It had just added former German colonies in Africa to its possessions. It was known as "the empire on which the sun never sets," since its territories were scattered around the globe.

## 2. Mongol Empire (~24 M km², around 1270-1309)

Founded by Genghis Khan, the Mongol Empire reached about 24 million km² between 1270 and 1309. It is the [largest contiguous empire in history](https://jwsr.pitt.edu/ojs/jwsr/article/view/369): its core, the Eurasian steppe, stretches from Manchuria to the Carpathians.

## 3. Russian Empire (~22.8 M km², 1895)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/empires/russian.svg" alt="Flag of Russian Empire" loading="lazy" />


The Russian Empire covered about 22.8 million km² in 1895, from Poland to the Pacific, after the conquest of Central Asia. Alaska, sold to the United States in 1867, was no longer part of it by then.

## 4. Qing Dynasty (~14.7 M km², around 1790)

China's last imperial dynasty reached about 14.7 million km² around 1790. Besides China itself, it then controlled Mongolia, Tibet and Eastern Turkestan.

## 5. Spanish Empire (~13.7 M km², around 1780-1810)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/empires/spanish.svg" alt="Flag of Spanish Empire" loading="lazy" />


The Spanish Empire covered about 13.7 million km² between 1780 and 1810, from the Americas to the Philippines, just before Latin America's independence. It is often described as the first global empire, present in both hemispheres.

## 6. Second French Colonial Empire (~11.5 M km², 1920)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/empires/french-colonial.svg" alt="Flag of Second French Colonial Empire" loading="lazy" />


The second French colonial empire reached about 11.5 million km² in 1920, after receiving part of German Cameroon and Togo. Its holdings were concentrated mainly in Africa and Indochina.

## 7. Abbasid Caliphate (~11.1 M km², around 750)
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/empires/abbasid.svg" alt="Flag of Abbasid Caliphate" loading="lazy" />


When the Abbasids took power in 750, the caliphate covered about 11.1 million km²: that was its maximum, inherited from the Umayyads. Baghdad, their capital, was founded in 762. The Abbasid Caliphate is associated with a scientific and intellectual golden age of the Muslim world, but its territory shrank from the end of the 8th century.

## 8. Umayyad Caliphate (~11.1 M km², around 720)

Some sixty years after its founding in 661, the Umayyad Caliphate already stretched from Spain to the Indus, covering about 11.1 million km² around 720. Few empires have grown so fast. Taagepera treats the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates as one and the same empire, hence their identical area.

## 9. Yuan Dynasty (~11 M km², around 1310)

The Yuan Dynasty, founded by Kublai Khan, grandson of Genghis Khan, covered about 11 million km² around 1310, in China and Mongolia. It was the largest of the states that emerged from the break-up of the Mongol Empire.

## 10. Xiongnu Empire (~9 M km², around 176 BC)

Born on the steppes of Central Asia, north of Han China, the Xiongnu Empire reached about 9 million km² [around 176 BC](https://jwsr.pitt.edu/ojs/jwsr/article/view/369). It stayed at that size for only about twenty years.

## What about the Portuguese Empire?
<img class="flag-badge" src="/flags/empires/portuguese.svg" alt="Flag of Portuguese Empire" loading="lazy" />


Some rankings credit the Portuguese Empire with more than 10 million km², Brazil included. Taagepera, who counts only territory under actual control, gives 5.5 million km² in 1820: Portugal then held only the coastal half of Brazil and a strip of Angola and Mozambique. It therefore does not make the top ten. It is, however, often cited as the longest-lasting of the colonial empires: from the capture of Ceuta in 1415 to the handover of Macau in 1999.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the largest empire in history?

The British Empire is the largest empire in history, with about 35.5 million km² at its peak in 1920. It then covered a little over a quarter of the world's land area outside Antarctica.

### What is the largest contiguous empire?

The Mongol Empire is the largest contiguous empire, with about 24 million km² between 1270 and 1309. Unlike the British Empire, scattered across the oceans, its territory formed a continuous block across Eurasia.

### Which empire lasted the longest?

The Roman Empire, if its Byzantine continuation is included, lasted about 1,500 years, from the end of the Roman Republic to the fall of Constantinople in 1453. Among colonial empires, the Portuguese is often cited as the most enduring, spanning nearly six centuries from Ceuta (1415) to Macau (1999).

### Where does the Roman Empire rank on this list?

The Roman Empire does not make the top 10 by area: it covered about 5 million km² at its peak, in 117, under Trajan. Its influence on law, languages and institutions nevertheless remains among the deepest in history.
