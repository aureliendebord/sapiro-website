We downloaded, played, and paid for ten quiz apps so you don't have to waste your time on the wrong one. Some were great. Some were rough.

Full disclosure: this comparison is published by the SAPIRO team, and SAPIRO is on the list. Prices and features of the other apps were checked against their official store listings and websites on 27 November 2026.

Here's what we found, rated on content quality, privacy, ad experience, educational value, and price. Whether you're a student, a parent hunting for screen time that actually teaches something, or just someone who likes knowing random facts about the world, one of these will fit. If you want the science behind why quizzes work for learning, our [guide to general knowledge](/en/blog/general-knowledge-guide/) covers spaced repetition and active recall in detail.

## 1. SAPIRO — The Complete Educational Quiz

SAPIRO covers geography, history, art, nature, and general culture through over 2,000 curated questions. Every answer comes with a real explanation, not just "correct" or "wrong," but the context behind it. The adaptive difficulty tracks your weak spots and adjusts, which genuinely helps over time rather than just letting you coast on what you already know.

The privacy angle is where SAPIRO pulls ahead of nearly everything else on this list. No ads at all. No data ever sold. No account required to start playing. For parents, that last part matters more than most app descriptions let on: you can hand your phone to a kid without worrying about what's being tracked or what ad they'll see next. The app holds a Teacher Approved badge on Google Play, and the user ratings back it up at 5 out of 5 stars.

The free version has plenty to work with and shows no ads. Offline mode works well, and the app supports English, French, and Spanish.

It's a newer app, so the question library is still growing, though it's already substantial.

**Price:** Free version, no ads.

## 2. Trivia Crack — The Social Trivia Classic

[Trivia Crack](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.etermax.preguntados.lite) is one of the most downloaded trivia games, with more than 100 million downloads on Google Play, and the spin-the-wheel mechanic across six categories (Entertainment, Art, Science, Sports, History, Geography) is still fun. Challenging friends or strangers keeps things competitive, and user-submitted questions mean the library is enormous.

The free version carries ads, though, and the App Store listing declares that data (purchases, identifiers, usage data) is used to track you for advertising. If that bothers you, steer clear.

On the learning side, there's not much to speak of. You find out if you got the answer right, but not why. And since players submit questions, quality varies.

Large player base, fun multiplayer, wide category range. But ads, ad tracking, and no explanations hold it back.

**Price:** Free with ads. A 365-day ad-free purchase costs €6.99 on the French App Store in November 2026; other extras via in-app purchases.

## 3. Kahoot! — The Classroom Quiz Platform

[Kahoot!](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=no.mobitroll.kahoot.android) turned classroom quizzes into live competitive events, and it works. A teacher creates a quiz, shares a PIN, students join from their devices. The music, leaderboards, and time pressure create real energy, as anyone who's been in a Kahoot! session knows.

The catch: Kahoot! is built around group play. The app does offer solo study modes (flashcards, self-paced challenges), but its main appeal is the hosted session. The free plan allows up to 10 participants, and [personal paid plans](https://kahoot.com/register/pricing-personal/) were listed at $3 to $19 per month, billed annually, on 27 November 2026 (prices recorded during a promotion).

Great for classrooms and group settings. Less suited for solo learning.

**Price:** Free plan (up to 10 participants). Personal plans from $3 to $19/month, billed annually, in November 2026.

## 4. Quizizz (now Wayground) — Quiz Platform for Schools

Quizizz, rebranded as [Wayground](https://wayground.com/home/plans) in 2025, covers similar ground to Kahoot! but adds asynchronous play: students complete quizzes at their own pace, which teachers appreciate. The reporting tools for tracking student progress are solid.

Same core limitation though: it is designed around teacher-selected content, even if students can study independently. If you're an individual learner, the whole experience feels like a classroom app that wasn't designed for you. The Basic plan is free for teachers; school and district plans are priced on request.

Good teacher analytics, flexible async mode. But firmly a classroom tool, not a solo learning app.

**Price:** Free Basic plan for teachers. School and district plans on request.

## 5. Erudite — Trivia With Rotating Circles

[Erudite](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.erudite.trivia.brain.quiz.mind.game) has its own mechanic: a main question sits in the centre, surrounded by nine sub-questions and nine answers arranged in circles, which you rotate to match each pair. You get three attempts per card. The app is translated into 19 languages.

The free version shows ads, and the App Store listing declares ad tracking. Ads can be removed through in-app purchases or subscriptions (for example €4.99 for seven days of VIP, or €14.99 for a lifetime PRO account on the French App Store in November 2026).

Original mechanic, wide language support. But ads in the free version, ad tracking, and subscriptions.

**Price:** Free with ads. In-app purchases.

## 6. Quiz Sans Fin (Endless Quiz) — Elo-Rated Trivia

[Quiz Sans Fin](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.timleg.quiz), called Endless Quiz in English, takes an interesting approach: it gives you an Elo number, as in chess, to track your progress and compare yourself with other players. Its listing mentions thousands of hand-picked questions, each linked to a Wikipedia article, letting you rabbit-hole into topics that catch your attention.

The app is available in six languages, including English and French. The Elo system is genuinely motivating if you're competitive, but there are no thematic learning paths, so your knowledge gains end up scattered rather than building toward anything. The free version shows ads, with ad tracking declared on the App Store.

The Elo mechanic is clever. But no structured learning, and explanations live on Wikipedia rather than in the app.

**Price:** Free with ads. No in-app purchases on the App Store.

## 7. GeoGuessr — Guess the Location

[GeoGuessr](https://www.geoguessr.com/) drops you into a random Google Street View location and asks: where are you? It's a brilliantly simple concept. You learn to read road signs, vegetation, architecture, sun position: the kind of observational geography no textbook teaches. The community is passionate and the game is genuinely addictive.

The scope is narrow by design: geography only, and specifically location-guessing. No capitals, no historical facts, no cultural knowledge. Free play is mostly limited to the daily challenge, and the subscription, at €6.99/month or about €48/year at list price in November 2026, is basically required. You also need a constant internet connection since it streams Street View imagery.

For a broader look at geography apps specifically, see our [comparison of the best geography apps](/en/blog/best-geography-apps/).

Unique, immersive, and teaches you to see the world differently. But geography-only, always-online, and free play is limited.

**Price:** Limited free play. Subscription €6.99/month or about €48/year in November 2026.

## 8. Seterra — Classic Map Quizzes

[Seterra](https://www.geoguessr.com/seterra) has been around since 1997. The format is simple: see a map, the app names a location, you click on it. For learning countries, capitals, flags, and physical geography, it just works. Spatial memory builds fast with this kind of drill.

That said, Seterra hasn't evolved much. The design feels dated, and the scope is strictly geography: no history, art, nature, or general culture. The mobile app is free, with no ads and no in-app purchases. No explanations for wrong answers.

Reliable and proven. But it's showing its age, and geography-only limits what you can get out of it.

**Price:** Free mobile app, no ads or in-app purchases, in November 2026.

## 9. QuizDuel — 1v1 Trivia Battles

[QuizDuel](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=se.maginteractive.quizduel2) (Quizduell in Germany, DuelQuiz in France) is head-to-head trivia against another player, played over six rounds. The publisher claims over 100 million players, so you always find a match, and there is also a solo mode.

The usual issues apply: ads in the free version, ad tracking declared on the App Store, and no explanations after answers. A 30-day ad-free purchase and a VIP subscription (€4.99/month on the French App Store in November 2026) remove the ads.

Fun duel format, big player base. But ads and no explanations limit the learning value.

**Price:** Free with ads. VIP subscription available.

## 10. StudyGe — Interactive Atlas and Quiz

[StudyGe](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mileoDev.geography) combines an interactive world atlas with geography quizzes covering countries, capitals, flags, and more. The atlas view is useful for visual learners who want to explore before testing themselves. On the French App Store in November 2026, removing ads costs €1.99 and the premium plan €29.99 per year.

Like several others here, StudyGe is geography-only. No history, art, nature, or broader knowledge. The quiz mechanics are straightforward without being memorable, and wrong answers don't come with explanations.

Solid atlas feature. But limited scope and no explanations.

**Price:** Free with ads. Premium €29.99/year.

## Our Verdict

The right app depends on what you actually need.

For live classroom energy, Kahoot! and Quizizz are hard to beat. For geography nerds who want something genuinely different, GeoGuessr is in a league of its own. For casual trivia with friends, Trivia Crack and QuizDuel have the player bases, but expect ads and ad tracking.

SAPIRO wins on the criteria that matter most for education and families: multi-domain content, real explanations, no ads, and no data ever sold. It's the only app on this list that covers geography, history, art, and nature in one place without selling your attention or your data. That combination doesn't exist elsewhere.

It's not the app for everyone. If you want multiplayer battles against strangers, SAPIRO now has a duel mode, but Trivia Crack has a far bigger pool. If you want location-guessing gameplay, GeoGuessr does something SAPIRO doesn't try to do. And if you need a classroom platform, Kahoot! was built for that.

But if you want to actually learn at your own pace, without ads and without your data being sold, SAPIRO is the strongest option we tested. The adaptive difficulty keeps it challenging as you improve, and the explanations mean you walk away knowing more, not just scoring higher.
