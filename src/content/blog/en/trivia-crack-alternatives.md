If the ads are what is pushing you away from Trivia Crack, the most direct replacement is SAPIRO: a general knowledge quiz with no ads, even in the free version, and an explanation after every answer. It is available on iOS and Android, and you can [play it free in your browser](/en/play/) without installing anything.

If what you actually loved was beating another human, QuizDuel is the closer match. You keep the duels, and you keep the ads too. Below are six options, with what each one does well and where it falls short.

## 1. SAPIRO: General Knowledge With No Ads

Let's get this out of the way: SAPIRO is our app, and you are reading this on our site. Weigh the rest accordingly.

SAPIRO covers four areas: geography (197 countries), history, art, and nature. There are over 2,000 verified questions, and each one comes with an explanation once you have answered. You don't just find out you were wrong, you find out why. There are no ads anywhere, free version included.

Besides the iOS and Android apps, you can [play in the browser](/en/play/) for free with no download. It is the quickest way to see whether the format suits you before putting anything on your phone.

What SAPIRO does not have is real-time matches against strangers, which is the heart of Trivia Crack. The social side runs through the Daily Challenge, which is free and identical for everyone. When you finish, you share your score with a link and dare a friend to beat it, and a leaderboard shows where you stand. It is a quieter experience than a wheel and six games running in parallel, and you should know that going in.

For the point-by-point breakdown, read [SAPIRO vs Trivia Crack](/en/blog/sapiro-vs-trivia-crack/).

## 2. QuizDuel: If You Miss the Duels

QuizDuel, sold as QuizClash in some markets, is the closest thing to Trivia Crack in spirit. It is head-to-head trivia, and players take turns picking the category, which adds a bit of strategy: play to your strengths, push your opponent into their weak spots. The player base is large, so finding a match is quick.

The drawbacks are probably the ones you are trying to escape. The free version runs frequent ads, and there are no explanations after the answers. Users also report opponents who play at inhuman speed, so you may be facing a bot more often than you would like.

## 3. Erudite: The Spin Wheel

If the wheel is the part of Trivia Crack you miss, Erudite uses a similar mechanic, with categories that include science, history, geography, and literature.

There is a serious catch for families. Multiple users have reported adult-themed ads inside the app. If ads are the reason you are leaving Trivia Crack, this is not an upgrade. The question library is also on the small side, so repeats show up after a couple of weeks of regular play.

## 4. Quiz Sans Fin: Trivia With a Chess-Style Rating

Quiz Sans Fin applies an Elo rating to trivia, the system used in chess. Your score rises or falls depending on how hard the questions were. Questions link out to Wikipedia, so you can go down a rabbit hole on anything that catches your eye.

The big caveat for most readers here: the app is primarily in French. It also has ads throughout, no themed learning paths, and no explanations inside the app itself. It is a clever idea that stays niche.

## 5. Kahoot!: For a Room Full of People

Kahoot! turned classroom quizzes into live events. Someone creates a quiz, shares a PIN, and everyone joins from their own device. The music, the leaderboard, and the time pressure create real energy.

It does not replace Trivia Crack for solo play on the train, though. Kahoot! is a platform, and it needs someone to create and host the quiz. For a party, a class, or a training session it is a strong pick. We compare the two approaches in [SAPIRO vs Kahoot](/en/blog/sapiro-vs-kahoot/).

## 6. Quizizz: Same Idea, at Your Own Pace

Quizizz covers similar ground to Kahoot! with one practical difference: participants complete quizzes at their own pace instead of all at once. The public quiz library is large but uneven.

The limitation is the same. It depends on content that a teacher created or picked, and as an individual player you may feel like you wandered into a classroom tool.

## What About QuizUp and HQ Trivia?

Both names still come up when people look for competitive trivia. QuizUp was built around real-time duels by category, and HQ Trivia ran live shows at a fixed time. The original QuizUp app shut down, and HQ Trivia closed before a few unstable attempts at a comeback. We cover both formats in [SAPIRO vs QuizUp and HQ Trivia](/en/blog/sapiro-vs-quizup-hq-trivia/), but we would not plan a daily habit around either.

## Which One Should You Pick?

**You are done with ads.** SAPIRO. It is the only one on this list with an ad-free free version. Start with a round [in the browser](/en/play/) and you will know within two minutes whether it is for you.

**You want to beat someone.** QuizDuel for pure duels, ads included. If a long-distance challenge is enough, SAPIRO's Daily Challenge does the job: everybody gets the same questions, you send your score, and your friend tries to top it.

**You are choosing for your kids.** Skip Erudite until the ad problem is fixed, and go with an ad-free app. Our list of the [best ad-free educational apps](/en/blog/best-ad-free-educational-apps/) has more options by subject.

**You like watching a rating climb.** Quiz Sans Fin, as long as you read French.

**You are running a group.** Kahoot! when everyone plays at once, Quizizz when people answer on their own schedule.
